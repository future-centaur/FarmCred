## MANIFEST — Kenyan smallholder agricultural credit, 2026-11-10, tier 1

Boundary: which facts about a farmer’s production, sales, cash, and repayment a lender must get right; which of those facts only size and structure the loan; which are pass/fail gates. Outside it: land-title search, running a cooperative or SACCO, designing insurance, agronomic advice, sex as a score, and the lender’s own books.

Research doc: `RESEARCH.md`, ranked **synthesis**. Regulatory rows below are also tied to primary instruments. The synthesis does not outrank those instruments where they differ.

### A. Entities

| id | canonical term | definition | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Smallholder | The farmer who seeks input credit on a small plot. | RESEARCH.md |
| A2 | Plot | The cultivated place, pinned by GPS at onboarding, not typed. | RESEARCH.md §0 |
| A3 | Ward | The administrative unit used for climate normals, county yield, and service area. Distinct from the plot pin. | RESEARCH.md §0, §6 |
| A4 | National identity | The legal identity the farmer states, read off an identity document, and compared with a registry record. | RESEARCH.md §0, identity section |
| A5 | Tenure | How the farmer holds the plot: owned, leased, family land, allotment letter, or title. A property detail. | RESEARCH.md §1; DPA 2019 s.2 “property details” |
| A6 | Crop | Primary and secondary crops on the plot, including intercrops. Also the financed-value-chain test. | RESEARCH.md §1, §9 |
| A7 | Production capacity | What the plot can physically produce. Sets a ceiling on how much can be repaid. It is not willingness to repay. | RESEARCH.md §1 |
| A8 | Yield | Physical output of a season, in kg or bags per acre. | RESEARCH.md §1; Part A #1 |
| A9 | Realized revenue | Cash received for that output, in KES, from a buyer or cooperative sales record. | RESEARCH.md §1; Part A #1 |
| A10 | Market linkage | The offtake relationship, from spot market only up to a signed offtake with a known buyer. | RESEARCH.md §2 |
| A11 | Delivery record | Volume and season-on-season consistency of produce actually delivered to a cooperative or processor. | RESEARCH.md §2; Part A #2 |
| A12 | Mobile-money cash flow | The pattern of inflows on the farmer’s mobile-money statement: level, irregularity, and months above a floor. | RESEARCH.md §3 |
| A13 | Debt-service ratio | Proposed instalment divided by median monthly inflow. | RESEARCH.md §3 |
| A14 | Off-farm income | Whether the household has income from outside the farm. Collected as a yes/no. An amount is optional and only for a borderline debt-service ratio. | RESEARCH.md Part A #4 |
| A15 | Loan exposure | Current outstanding debt stock: balance and count, across SACCO, bank, and mobile lender. | RESEARCH.md §4; Part A #5 |
| A16 | Repayment performance | How past credit was paid, from SACCO, PAYGo, agro-dealer, or chama records. Distinct from exposure. | RESEARCH.md §4; Part A #5 |
| A17 | Credit-reference listing | Adverse or other customer information held by a credit reference bureau. A bureau “credit score” is this listing’s number, not the farm evidence score. | CRB Regulations LN 55 of 2020, reg 18; RESEARCH.md Part A #6 |
| A18 | PAYGo account | Asset-finance repayment on solar, a water pump, or a cookstove. Evidence of small regular payments. A financed device can also evidence irrigation. | RESEARCH.md §4, PAYGo section |
| A19 | Chama | An informal group or table-banking arrangement. Membership is not the same fact as how the person repaid inside it. | RESEARCH.md §5; Part A #11 |
| A20 | SACCO savings balance | The stock of savings held at a cooperative. Not the same instrument as mobile-wallet savings frequency. | RESEARCH.md §5; Part A #3 |
| A21 | Mobile-wallet savings frequency | How often the farmer moves money into savings on mobile money. A flow, not a balance. | RESEARCH.md §3; Part A #3 |
| A22 | Group guarantee | A person or a group who agrees to vouch. The person is named by the farmer. The group confirms. | RESEARCH.md §4, §5 |
| A23 | Reference contact | A cooperative official, agrovet, or chama chair the farmer chooses to name. Entered by hand. | RESEARCH.md §5; Part B |
| A24 | Climate exposure | Historical rainfall reliability and drought or flood history for the ward. Used when the loan is structured. | RESEARCH.md §6; Part A #7 |
| A25 | NDVI anomaly | This season’s vegetation on this plot compared with that plot’s own baseline. An in-season monitoring signal. | RESEARCH.md §6; Part A #7 |
| A26 | Rainfall deficit | Season-to-date rain against the ward’s long-run average. An in-season monitoring signal. | RESEARCH.md §6; Part A #7 |
| A27 | Irrigation access | Whether the plot is irrigated. One fact, used by production capacity and by climate structuring. | RESEARCH.md Part A #8 |
| A28 | Eligibility gate | A pass/fail condition that is not awarded points and is not traded off against a strong score. | RESEARCH.md §9, design framework |
| A29 | Input loan | Credit whose purpose and repayment sit on a planting cycle. Distinct from a general cash loan. | RESEARCH.md §8 |
| A30 | Expected harvest date | When cash from the crop is agronomically expected. | RESEARCH.md §8; Part A #10 |
| A31 | Preferred repayment month | The month the borrower asks to repay. | RESEARCH.md §8; Part A #10 |
| A32 | Field attestation | A field agent or agronomist confirming a ground-truth claim the farmer’s phone cannot prove, including tenure. | RESEARCH.md developer insights; Jumba note |
| A33 | Consent | Express, unequivocal, free, specific, and informed agreement for a named purpose. One checkbox does not cover every category. | DPA 2019 s.2, s.32; RESEARCH.md Part B, Part C |
| A34 | Sensitive personal data | Includes property details, marital status, family details (names of children, parents, spouse), and sex. | DPA 2019 s.2 |

### B. Resolved conflicts

| id | the conflict | resolution | resolution type | if violated, what breaks and for whom |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Yield (volume) vs realized revenue (KES) | Keep both. Yield is self-report or extension-verified at harvest. Revenue is the buyer or cooperative sales record. The gap is diagnostic of loss or weak prices. | keep both distinct | A farmer with crop in the store and no buyer is treated as able to repay. The lender over-advances; the farmer is sized on grain that never becomes cash. |
| B2 | Market linkage as a category vs as volume and consistency | Sequence them. The category (spot market only through signed offtake) is collected at onboarding. Volume and consistency become the scored metric once at least one season of verifiable delivery exists. With no history, fall back to the category plus acreage times county-average yield. | sequence them | A new applicant is blocked for having no delivery history, or a signed offtake with no deliveries is scored as if produce moved. The thin-file farmer or the lender’s limit is wrong. |
| B3 | Mobile-wallet savings frequency vs SACCO savings balance | Keep both. One is a mobile-money flow. The other is a cooperative stock. Do not average them into one savings number. | keep both distinct | A farmer with a SACCO balance and no wallet habit, or the reverse, gets a blended score that matches neither instrument. Pricing and limit miss the real buffer. |
| B4 | Off-farm income as an amount vs a yes/no | Default to yes/no. Ask an amount only if the answer is yes and a borderline debt-service ratio needs it. The amount is self-reported and optional. | sequence them | The farmer is pushed to disclose an exact side income before anyone can say it changes the ratio, or a debt-service ratio is computed as if side income were known when it was not asked. |
| B5 | Loan exposure vs repayment performance | Keep both. Exposure is self-reported debt stock, corroborated by the bureau where consented. Performance is SACCO, PAYGo, and agro-dealer records, each with its own consent. | keep both distinct | Current debt is read as “pays well,” or a clean payment history hides a full existing book. Over-indebted farmers are approved, or proven payers are declined. |
| B6 | Bureau listing as points and as an automatic knockout | A routine listing is a bounded scored input, not an automatic knockout. Weight only negative listings above the KES 1,000 line and inside a recency window whose length is still open (C7). Hard gates are identity fraud and active insolvency, plus the area and crop gates in D5. | collapse into one | A small or stale default cancels an otherwise sound farm file, or the same listing is punished twice, once in points and once as a gate. Borderline farmers are declined twice for one fact. |
| B7 | Regional climate exposure vs in-season NDVI and rainfall deficit | Keep both. The regional score structures the loan at origination: size, term, whether insurance is mandatory. NDVI anomaly and rainfall deficit monitor the book after disbursement. They are not inputs to the accept or decline. | keep both distinct | A drought-prone ward declines a farmer who delivers and repays, or an in-season stress signal is ignored until default. Whole areas are excluded, or the lender restructures too late. |
| B8 | Irrigation listed under production and under climate | Capture once. Refer to that same fact from both views. Do not ask twice. | collapse into one | The farmer is asked again, the two answers diverge, and one module structures the loan on a different water fact than the other. |
| B9 | Tenure, off-farm income, and years on the plot listed under the farm profile and under resilience | Capture each once at onboarding. Any module that needs it reads that value. | collapse into one | Two copies drift, and resilience scoring uses a different tenure or income flag than the farm profile the offer was based on. |
| B10 | Expected harvest date vs preferred repayment month | Keep both. Reconcile at structuring. Use the harvest date to check the requested month. Do not treat one as the other. | keep both distinct | Repayment is scheduled on a month the farmer preferred and the harvest does not fund, or the farmer’s stated month is discarded and the instalment lands before cash exists. |
| B11 | Group membership vs group repayment performance | Keep both. Membership facts (name, member number, join date) are self-reported at onboarding. Performance is pulled later with the cooperative’s consent. | keep both distinct | Years in a chama are scored as good repayment, or a strong payer who just joined is scored as a long member. The vouch is about the wrong fact. |

### C. Open questions

| id | question | why it matters | what would settle it |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Does household size or a dependency ratio enter the file, as an aggregate count only? | Repayment capacity is currently individual income and assets. Dependents change what is left for the instalment. Names of family members stay out either way (D3). | A practitioner or CGAP-aligned credit policy says whether the count changes limit or price, and a collection test shows farmers will answer a count. |
| C2 | Is post-harvest loss collected as its own parameter? | The yield–revenue gap is diagnostic of loss, but loss itself is not on the file. Unmeasured loss overstates cash available to repay. | A buyer, cooperative, or field protocol that records storage and loss separately from the sales receipt, and a decision on whether that record changes the limit. |
| C3 | Does existing crop or index insurance uptake change structuring? | Insurance already in force changes how tightly climate must size the loan and whether further cover is mandatory. | A named insurer or index product, and a rule for how an in-force policy changes size, term, or the insurance requirement. |
| C4 | Is extension or advisory engagement a diligence signal? | Some scoring models treat training or SMS advisory uptake as diligence, separate from seed, fertilizer, and yield. | A source the lender can actually receive, and evidence it predicts repayment beyond input package and yield. |
| C5 | Does distance to input suppliers and markets enter the file? | Access changes whether inputs arrive on time and whether the market linkage is real. | A definition of distance the file can compute from the plot pin, and a decision on whether it changes linkage score or loan size. |
| C6 | Are phone type and digital literacy in scope? | They decide which of the other parameters can be collected. They are not themselves creditworthiness. | A decision that feature-phone farmers follow a defined shorter path, or that phone type stays off the score and only routes the journey. |
| C7 | How long is the credit-reference recency window? | Part A #6 requires a window and only illustrates 24 months. Without a length, B6 cannot be applied. | The lender’s credit policy, or a CBK instrument, stating the months of negative information that may be weighted. |
| C8 | What are the score bands and weights? | The research says most scored inputs should sit in 3–5 bands and be explainable to a credit committee. No bands or weights are set. | A credit-policy sheet with bands, weights, and the two axes (structuring vs creditworthiness; points vs gates). |
| C9 | What is the serviceable area, and which value chains are financed? | Both are eligibility gates. Undefined gates either decline everyone or no one. | The lender names the wards and the crops. |
| C10 | May sex be stored for fairness monitoring? | The Act makes sex sensitive personal data. The research keeps gender out of the score and wants it for bias checks. Storage still needs a permitted ground. | A permitted ground under DPA s.45, accepted by the controller’s DPIA, or a decision to not store it at all. |
| C11 | Must this controller register with the ODPC regardless of revenue? | DPA s.18 requires registration subject to thresholds the Commissioner prescribes. The research states that financial services register regardless of size. Those are not the same rule. | The ODPC registration regulations or a Commissioner notice naming this industry and whether a turnover floor applies. |
| C12 | Do the Credit Reference Bureau Regulations 2020 remain in force? | The KES 1,000 submission ban sits in those regulations. The High Court nullified them; the Court of Appeal suspended that nullification pending appeal (KECA 101 of 2024). | The Court of Appeal’s final decision in Nairobi Civil Appeal E782 of 2023, or a replacement instrument. |
| C13 | Who may lawfully obtain the bureau report: the operator, or only a licensed lender? | The journey ends with a bureau check. Digital-credit-provider duties apply to a provider, not automatically to a scoring vendor. Banks and SACCOs sit under other regimes. | The operator’s licensing path: digital credit provider, bank or microfinance institution, SASRA SACCO, or vendor to one of those. |
| C14 | What fact counts as active insolvency for the hard gate? | B6 reserves a knockout for active insolvency and not for a routine default. The regulations’ “negative information” includes bankruptcies, liquidations, and receiverships, which is wider than one phrase. | The lender maps “active insolvency” onto specific bureau codes or a court status, and says what happens to other negative-information types. |
| C15 | How long is applicant data kept when no loan is made? | DPA s.25(g) forbids keeping identifiable data longer than necessary. No schedule exists. | A retention schedule: declined and abandoned applications versus booked loans. |
| C16 | Which vendors and fields may leave Kenya? | DPA s.25(h) requires adequate safeguards or consent for a cross-border transfer. Satellite, cloud, and model vendors may sit outside Kenya. No localization notice was checked. | A vendor list with country, the safeguard or consent for each, and any Cabinet Secretary localization notice that covers a field in this file. |

### D. Constraints

| id | constraint | source | if violated, what breaks and for whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Personal data is limited to what the stated purpose needs, collected for an explicit purpose, and kept identifiable only as long as that purpose requires. | DPA 2019 s.25(c), (d), (g) | Side income, contacts, or old applications are held “just in case.” Farmers who never borrow stay on file, and a later breach or misuse exposes them. |
| D2 | Consent is specific to a purpose. The controller has to be able to show it. Withdrawing it stops later processing and does not undo processing already done. Conditioning the loan on data the loan does not need is evidence consent was not free. | DPA 2019 s.2, s.32 | A single “I agree” covers the bureau, mobile money, the cooperative, and GPS. A farmer cannot refuse one category without refusing the product, including categories the research marks optional. |
| D3 | Marital status is not collected. Names of a spouse, children, or parents are not collected. If household size is added later (C1), it is an aggregate count. | DPA 2019 s.2 “sensitive personal data”; RESEARCH.md Part B | Family details sit on a credit file. That is sensitive personal data processed without a proportionate purpose, and it exposes the household, not only the applicant. |
| D4 | Sex is not a scoring input. Whether it may be stored for fairness monitoring is C10. | DPA 2019 s.2 (sex is sensitive); RESEARCH.md Part B | Women are scored down through a gender field, on top of asset fields that already skew male because of how land and livestock are registered. |
| D5 | The hard gates are failed identity verification, active insolvency (definition open, C14), plot outside the serviceable area (C9), and crop outside the financed value chains (C9). A routine bureau default is not a hard gate (B6). | RESEARCH.md §9 as amended by Part A #6 | Identity fraud is funded, or a drought ward or a small default is used as if it were fraud. Viable farmers are excluded, or the lender books a file with no identity. |
| D6 | Climate exposure, NDVI anomaly, and rainfall deficit size, term, insure, or trigger monitoring. They do not decline an application. “Outside serviceable area” (D5) is coverage, not climate. | RESEARCH.md §6, Part A #7, weather note | A disciplined farmer in a dry ward is declined for geography. The book excludes whole regions. |
| D7 | Negative credit information whose amount does not exceed KES 1,000 is not submitted to a bureau. A listing at or under that line does not influence this score. The Credit Reference Bureau Regulations are in force only while the Court of Appeal’s suspension of the High Court nullification holds (C12). Digital credit providers have the same submission ban in their own regulations. | CRB Regulations LN 55 of 2020, reg 18(2); CBK v Bogongo KECA 101 (2024); DCP Regulations LN 46 of 2022, reg 14(3); RESEARCH.md Part A #6 and Part B | A sub-1,000 listing lowers the score or declines the farmer. People the regulations told bureaus not to receive are still punished. |
| D8 | Before a digital credit provider submits negative information, it notifies the customer at least thirty days beforehand. This is separate from notice of a report pulled at decision time, which the journey places last and in plain language. | DCP Regulations LN 46 of 2022, reg 14(5); RESEARCH.md Part C phase 6 | The farmer first learns of a negative listing when it has already been sent, or meets a bureau pull at the start of onboarding with no explanation. |
| D9 | In debt collection, a digital credit provider does not access the customer’s phone book or contacts, and does not call or message those contacts without authority. This product does not read a phone book at any point, including scoring. A reference contact or guarantor is typed by the farmer. | DCP Regulations LN 46 of 2022, reg 20(c), (e); RESEARCH.md Part B | Contacts are scraped “for the score” and later used to chase a debt. People who never applied are contacted. This is the harm the regulation names. |
| D10 | A provider collects only customer information reasonably required for appraisal, approval, disbursement, and collection. | DCP Regulations LN 46 of 2022, reg 26(1); applies if C13 resolves to a digital credit provider | The file accumulates contacts, family names, and exact side income that the decision does not use. |
| D11 | Tenure, title or ownership, and livestock are property details. They need their own express consent, not the general onboarding consent. GPS is explained as the key to satellite and weather for this application, and is not reused for another purpose. | DPA 2019 s.2, s.44, s.45; RESEARCH.md Part B | Land and animals are collected under a vague accept, or the plot pin is reused beyond crop and weather. The farmer’s property detail is on a file they did not specifically allow. |
| D12 | A processing operation likely to result in high risk to the data subject requires a data protection impact assessment before processing starts. This product’s research treats large-scale automated profiling for a credit decision as that case (assumption E3). | DPA 2019 s.31; RESEARCH.md Part B | Scoring goes live, then the assessment is written around a model that already decided people. The people already scored cannot be unprotected retroactively. |
| D13 | Identifiable personal data does not leave Kenya unless there are adequate safeguards or the data subject’s consent for that transfer. | DPA 2019 s.25(h) | Satellite, cloud, or model vendors process the file abroad with no safeguard and no specific consent. |
| D14 | No third-party credit information provider furnishes a customer’s credit information to a bureau except with the customer’s prior written consent. | CRB Regulations LN 55 of 2020, reg 23(6), subject to C12 | Cooperative, agro-dealer, or PAYGo performance is forwarded into the bureau file without written consent. |
| D15 | Absence of a cooperative delivery record is not an adverse mark. A remote-sensing series that is missing or timed out is not a zero. | RESEARCH.md co-op profiles; NDVI note | Non-members and cloudy plots are scored as bad farmers. Thin files and sensor gaps become declines. |
| D16 | There is no land-registry title lookup. Tenure stays self-report plus field attestation. | RESEARCH.md Jumba note | The build waits on a registry that does not serve most smallholders, and untitled farmers are stuck. |
| D17 | Agro-input history is derived from mobile-money counterparties mapped to known dealers, plus an optional receipt photo. It is not a live dealer API. | RESEARCH.md agro-input section | The score waits on a supplier API the research says the sector does not offer, or SKU-level seed and fertilizer claims are treated as known from a statement line that does not carry them. |
| D18 | The two decision axes stay visible: production capacity and climate structure the loan; market linkage, cash flow, repayment performance, and social collateral speak to whether repayment happens once the harvest exists. Points and gates are not interchangeable. | RESEARCH.md design framework | One blended number both declines and sizes, and a committee cannot see which question failed. |

### E. Declared assumptions

| id | assumption | why relied on | what would confirm or refute it |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | A KIAMIS lookup can return farmer id, national id, ward, parcel geometry, acreage, crops, livestock count, and validation status. | The research says that is what the registry holds, and background enrichment depends on it. No Ministry data dictionary was opened. | A KIAMIS data dictionary or an MoU sample file. A field the registry does not hold refutes the row. |
| E2 | A negative listing at or under KES 1,000, if it appears in a file anyway, does not change the score. | The regulations ban submission. The research also bans influence on the score. The second sentence is wider than the submission ban, and it is the rule the scorer must implement. | A compliance reading that use of an already-held sub-1,000 listing is permitted would refute the scoring half. A worked example where such a listing leaves both points and the gate unchanged would confirm the implementation. |
| E3 | This processing is “likely to result in high risk” under DPA s.31, so the impact assessment is a precondition, not a follow-up. | The Act states the test. The research applies the test to this credit profiling. No ODPC determination on this product was read. | The controller’s DPIA, or ODPC guidance, agreeing or disagreeing that this operation meets the test. |
| E4 | Some party in the journey can lawfully obtain a bureau report at the last step. | The journey and B6 assume a report exists to read. C13 is open, so the assumption is only that a licensed party, not necessarily this operator, can obtain it. | C13 resolved, plus the licence or agency agreement that authorizes the pull. |
